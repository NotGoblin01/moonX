Concept 01

1. What is a Planetary Analog?

A planetary analog is a natural location on Earth that shares physical, geological, chemical, or operational characteristics with environments found on other celestial bodies, such as the Moon or Mars.

No single location on Earth is an identical replica of the Moon or Mars. However, specific extreme environments—such as hyper-arid deserts, glaciated volcanic plateaus, meteorite impact craters, and sub-zero polar valleys—exhibit isolated traits that closely resemble extraterrestrial conditions.

💡
Key takeaway: When scientists call a location an "analog," they mean it models specific target properties (e.g., razor-sharp basaltic rocks, freezing vacuum-like dryness, or crater wall steepness), rather than an entire extraterrestrial planet.
Application 02

2. Why Are Earth Analogs Useful?

Sending hardware, rovers, and human crews into space is exceptionally expensive and unforgiving of failure. Testing prototypes in realistic terrestrial environments dramatically lowers mission risk and development costs.

🚜

Hardware & Rover Testing

Robotic wheels, rocker-bogie suspensions, and drills encounter fine abrasive sand, jagged volcanic basalt, and steep scree before flight builds are frozen.

🧑‍🚀

Astronaut Field Training

Crews practice geology sampling, extravehicular activity (EVA) suit ergonomics, and communication protocols in remote, demanding terrain.

🔬

Instrument Calibration

Spectrometers, ground-penetrating radar (GPR), and volatile detection sensors calibrate against actual evaporites, clays, and permafrost ice.

Platform 03

3. What is MoonBase Planner?

MoonBase Planner is an interactive web-based decision support system designed to systematically evaluate and match candidate Lunar and Martian sites with Earth analog environments.

Instead of relying on fragmented research papers, MoonBase Planner pairs target candidate regions (such as Shackleton Crater, Mare Tranquillitatis, or Jezero Crater) with quantified Earth sites using standardized physical parameters, dual algorithmic scoring, and transparent mission suitability classifications.

Scoring Logic 05

5. How Does the Analog Score Work?

MoonBase Planner calculates an overall similarity score on an intuitive scale from 0 to 100 using a transparent, weighted multi-attribute model.

Before computing scores, each physical variable is normalized to a continuous scale between 0.0 and 1.0. We calculate individual feature similarity percentages using bounded normalized distance functions:

Feature_Similarity_Score (%) = (1.0 - |Target_Normalized - Earth_Normalized|) × 100

Machine Learning 06

6. How Does the ML Similarity Model Work?

In addition to the rule-weighted prototype score, MoonBase Planner implements an unsupervised K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) & Cosine Distance Vector Space model.

Step 1

Vector Construction

Every Earth analog and planetary target is transformed into a 6-dimensional normalized vector: [slope, ruggedness, min_temp, max_temp, aridity, rock_fraction].

Step 2

Euclidean Distance Computation

The model calculates standard Euclidean geometric distance in $\mathbb{R}^6$ to measure physical multidimensional proximity.

Step 3

Cosine Similarity Alignment

Cosine angle metric evaluates how proportionally balanced the environmental traits are relative to the target body.

Step 4

Ranked Proximity Index

Returns the closest statistical neighbors alongside Euclidean distance measurements in the API response.

🛡️
Critical Scientific Guardrail: The ML model measures environmental feature proximity. It is explicitly NOT a prediction of biological habitability or alien life support.
Mission Strategy 07

7. Mission Recommendation Engine

Based on the physical profile of an analog, our recommendation engine classifies potential operational uses into 6 standardized categories:

  • Rover testing: Topography with high ruggedness, steep gradient walls, and loose gravel for traction slip trials.
  • Astronaut field training: Expansive multi-kilometer crater valleys and exposed unweathered rock for EVA traverses.
  • Equipment testing: Sub-zero cryogenic thermal regimes and abrasive airborne dust to stress joint seals and batteries.
  • Geological research: Hydrothermal deposits, impact breccia, and unweathered basalts for sampling protocols.
  • Navigation testing: High-contrast shadow fields and GPS-denied subsurface lava tubes for LiDAR SLAM.
  • Surface operations: Planar basins for inflatable base camp deployment and In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) tests.
📌
Guardrail Wording: All recommendations strictly apply forward-looking phrasing: "Potentially useful for...", "Could support...", or "Suitable for prototype testing based on the selected criteria." We do not claim any official space agency certification.
Scientific Rigor 08

8. What Are the Limitations?

Rigorous science requires being explicit about what an algorithm cannot do:

Atmospheric Pressure & Gravity

Earth has a 1-bar nitrogen-oxygen atmosphere and 1.0g gravity. The Moon has a near-total vacuum and 0.166g; Mars has ~6 mbar of CO2 and 0.38g. Terrestrial aerodynamics and projectile ballistic trajectories differ significantly.

Radiation Environment

Earth's magnetosphere and thick atmosphere shield the surface from galactic cosmic rays (GCRs) and solar particle events (SPEs). Earth analogs cannot recreate lunar or martian ionizing radiation doses.

Chemical Weathering & Biology

Earth has rain, liquid water cycles, and microbial biology that naturally weather rock surfaces into clays over geological time, whereas lunar regolith is pulverized by micrometeorites in absolute dryness.

Spatial Resolution Disparities

Earth elevation models (30m SRTM) and lunar datasets (100m LOLA) differ in spatial resolution, meaning micro-scale boulder distributions require localized rover-scale imagery.

Roadmap 09

9. What Could Be Improved in Future?

01

Sub-Meter Terrain Ingestion

Incorporate ultra-high resolution LiDAR point clouds (e.g., USGS 3DEP 1m elevation and NASA HiRISE 25cm imagery) for boulder-level obstacle avoidance.

02

Thermal-Vacuum & UV Simulation

Pair terrestrial analog locations with chamber test parameters (e.g., cryogenic thermal vac ratings) for complete environmental envelopes.

03

User-Defined Weight Fine-Tuning

Allow mission planners to adjust importance sliders (e.g., 50% weight on cold temperature for Artemis polar drill testing) directly from the dashboard.

04

Expanded Catalog Expansion

Ingest verified space agency analog field sites across Australia, South Africa, Oman, and the Tibetan Plateau.

10. Dedicated Space-Agency Data Sources

Every dataset integrated into MoonBase Planner originates from official national space agency archives and validated scientific institutions. We do not invent URLs, variables, or citations. Below is the complete transparent audit registry:

NASA Goddard Space Flight Center (GSFC) / NASA PDS Public Domain • NASA Open Data

Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO) — LOLA GDR Digital Elevation Model

Official Archive: https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/missions/lro/lola.htm ↗
Used For

Topographic elevation mapping, calculation of lunar crater wall slopes, and micro-relief Terrain Ruggedness Index (TRI) modeling.

Relevant Variables

elevation_m (radial elevation vs R=1737.4 km sphere), slope_mean_deg, slope_max_deg, terrain_ruggedness_index_m.

Resolution & Coverage

Up to 512–1024 pixels/degree (~30m to 60m/pixel at polar cold traps); continuous global mapping (2009–present).

License & Usage

NASA Open Data Policy / US Government Public Domain (Free and open for research, educational, and commercial application).

Official Archive: https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/missions/lro/diviner.htm ↗
